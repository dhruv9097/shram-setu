Problem Statement 1 — Migrant Worker Tracking

eShram knows who India’s workers are. Not where they are.

It records a worker once at enrolment, against a home address. They migrate; the record stays. Welfare goes there and never arrives.

So this is not tracking. It is presence the worker switches on, because it pays them back.

The inversion

Why a tracking app fails

  • Low literacy, twenty languages. Text-heavy screens exclude the people they serve.
  • One in five has no smartphone.
  • Worksites have no signal.
  • No reason to update a database that follows you.

What the worker gets back

  • Ration that travels with them.
  • Proof of employment for a claim or the next job.
  • Warnings in the language they read.
  • Control. Consent is per purpose, revocable, and every lookup is logged where they can see it.

Privacy architecture

Officials see flows. Seeing a person needs a reason.

  • District granularity. Coordinates verify a worksite boundary, then are discarded after seven days.
  • Suppression below ten. A single withheld cell can be recovered by subtraction, so it takes a neighbour with it.
  • Gated and logged. Seeing an individual needs a recorded basis, and they can read who looked.

Working prototype, not a mockup

Built solo, and running today

  • One deployable system. Next.js and PostgreSQL. The worker app installs as a PWA — no app store.
  • Real geography. 726 districts, 14 corridors from Census and Economic Survey data.
  • Honest boundary. eShram UAN auth sits behind a mocked interface. No Aadhaar data stored.
  • Verified, not asserted. Privacy guarantees and the offline queue ship with tests.
